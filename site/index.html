Fish

A small ecosystem: ten sea animals with their own AI brains over a seafloor of plants. They start as tiny fry, eat, grow and evolve into squid, turtles, sharks, rays, orcas and whales, make friends and enemies, hunt and fight. Tap the screen to feed them.

Hello Screen

Lights up the screen: a greeting, colour bars and a seconds counter.

Blink

The two lights beside the screen take turns. The screen stays blank.

How to flash, step by step
  1. Plug the board into the computer with the mini-USB cable (the socket marked USB ST-LINK).
  2. Click Flash beside the program you want.
  3. Chrome shows a small pop-up. Click STM32 STLink in its list, then the pop-up's Connect button.
  4. Wait a few seconds for “Done”. The new program starts by itself.

How the ecosystem works
  • Plants grow on the seafloor, faster when the water is rich. Grown plants release food. The two smallest species also nibble the plants.
  • Evolving: everyone starts as a tiny Fry. Eating earns meal points; enough of them and an animal evolves: Fry → Squid → Turtle, then Shark → Orca if it has been a hunter, or Ray → Whale if it has been gentle. Each step is bigger and stronger.
  • Hunting: a hungry animal hunts clearly weaker ones. Fighting: enemies attack each other whatever their size, and both get hurt. Every bite is also a meal for the biter.
  • Size matters: the bigger an animal, the harder it bites and the more health it has. Hunters (Shark, Orca) bite harder still; the gentle giants (Ray, Whale) are tougher and can graze on plants.
  • Relationships: swimming together builds friendship; crowding, stealing food and attacks make enemies. Friends never attack each other.
  • The cycle: when an animal dies, what's left enriches the water, which grows the plants, which feed the animals.
  • Personality: every animal has five traits in its genes: boldness, sociability, aggression, loyalty and curiosity. They change what it does (loyal animals never attack their own family, aggressive ones fight over smaller grudges) and its brain can sense them.
  • Genetics: the animal doing best has a baby in the dead one's place, with its best friend (or the next best animal). The baby gets a mix of both parents' genes, including their brains, with a few mutations, and joins its mum's family.

How they talk

The animals speak with our own language model, built from scratch and trained on GitHub. It writes one character at a time, and it's small enough to run on the board itself. Each time, it's told what just happened, what kind of animal is speaking, its personality, and how it feels about who it's talking to; then the other animal replies, having heard it. It learned from 120,000 practice lines written for each species and personality.

    Evolution

    Every animal starts as a Fry and evolves by eating. After the Turtle the path splits: animals that have hunted become Sharks and then Orcas; gentle ones become Rays and then Whales. Bright animals are in the tank now.

    The gene pool over time

    For each gene, the line is the average across the animals alive at the time and the shaded band runs from the lowest to the highest. When a line drifts up or down, the tank is evolving: animals with those genes are surviving and having babies more than the rest.

    Genes and family

    Every animal carries a genome: genes it is born with and keeps for life. A baby gets each gene from one parent or the other, and now and then a gene changes a little on the way (a mutation). So families look and behave alike, and over time the tank drifts towards whatever genes help animals survive and have babies.

    Body and personality

    The bar is this animal; M and D mark its mum's and dad's values. The first four genes shape its body; the five marked “trait” are its personality, which it can also sense, so it shapes how its brain behaves.

    Brain genes

    Its brain is part of its genome too: one stripe per connection strength (blue positive, orange negative, stronger = darker). A baby gets each hidden cell, with all its connections, whole from one parent, like a chromosome.

    Families

    The tank starts with ten founders, each the head of a family. Every baby joins its mum's family. Each chart shows how many of that family are alive over time (all on the same scale, 0 to 10); families that lose every member die out for good.

    Inside a brain

    Every animal in the tank is steered by its own small neural network. This is one of them, thinking, right now: what it senses on the left (including its own personality), two layers of hidden cells (16, then 8) in the middle, and what it decides on the right.

    Blue: a positive signal ("more", "yes", "to the right") Orange: a negative signal ("less", "no", "to the left") Brighter and thicker = stronger right now

    Each line is one connection. Its strength is fixed for that animal's whole life; babies get their parent's connections with small random changes, which is how the brains evolve.
